The color scheme decides *light or dark*. The new **Theme** row in **Settings → Appearance** decides *whose colors*. Every theme has both, so **System** still follows your Mac or PC through the day.

| Theme | Dark | Light | Accent |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Files** | Files' own | Files' own | yours: Iris, Azure or Amber |
| **Omarchy** | Tokyo Night | Tokyo Night Day | its own blue |
| **Catppuccin** | Mocha | Latte | Iris → mauve, Azure → blue, Amber → peach |

## Omarchy's terminal look

Omarchy is not only colors. With it, Files looks like a well-made terminal app: a monospace font everywhere (Menlo on macOS, Consolas on Windows, DejaVu Sans Mono on Linux), square corners, no shadows, and borders you can see — boxes drawn with lines, like a TUI.

<Callout title="Tip">
<Kbd>⌘</Kbd> <Kbd>⇧</Kbd> <Kbd>T</Kbd> flips between light and dark and keeps the theme: Tokyo Night becomes Tokyo Night Day, Mocha becomes Latte.
</Callout>

## Icons

The **Icons** row next to it changes how file and folder icons are colored, everywhere — lists, the preview, Mail, archives:

- **Colorful** — a color per kind: folders, pictures, code, archives…
- **Monochrome** — one quiet color, folders a little brighter.
- **Accent** — everything in the accent color.

Each button shows a sample, and the theme buttons show three dots of their colors, so you see the result before pressing Save.
