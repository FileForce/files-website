## Open first, read after

Quick Look used to read the file before it showed anything — a big archive or document meant a pause after <Kbd>Space</Kbd>. Now the window opens **immediately** with *Loading preview…*, the file is read in the background, and the preview fills in when it is ready. The second time, it is instant. ⌘K works the same way: the window opens at once and its index is built in the background — usually before you ever press it, a few seconds after Files starts.

## Documents you can scroll

Word and PowerPoint previews used to be a picture of the first page. Files now reads the text of **.docx**, **.odt** and **.pptx** itself — on every system, without Office — and Quick Look scrolls through the whole document. Presentations come slide by slide:

```text
— Slide 1 —
Quarterly results

— Slide 2 —
Revenue grew 18%
```

The preview panel shows the beginning of the document instead of a placeholder.

<Callout>
It is the text, not the layout: pictures and tables are left out. Old .doc and .ppt files still show the system's picture of the first page.
</Callout>
